The changelog tells you *what* shipped. This blog tells you *why*, *how*, and *what it felt like* to get there. Deep dives, launch notes, and honest postmortems from the person building Seaquel.

## Why start a blog now?

For the last three months, every meaningful decision I've made about Seaquel has lived in private docs, Discord/Bluesky DMs, half-finished drafts, or my whiteboard. That's a shame, because most of what I think about — query planning, local-first sync, the weird edge cases of drag-and-drop in a table with 50 million rows — is the *good* stuff.

So I'm opening those notebooks up.

## What to expect

Posts will fall into a few rough buckets:

- **Engineering deep dives** — How I built something, what I tried first, what broke along the way and why.
- **Product notes** — Why I made a particular decision, what I rejected, and what I'm still unsure about.
- **Launches and recaps** — When I ship something big enough to warrant more than a changelog section.
- **The occasional essay** — Opinions about databases, tools, and the craft of making developer software.

## What this isn't

This won't be SEO bait. It won't be "10 Tips to Write Better SQL." It won't be written by a content team because there isn't one 😅. It also won't be written by AI, despite the use of the Em dash `—`. Every post comes from Mike, hand-crafted on a laptop, likely at a coffee shop or library.

If that sounds like something you'd like to read, subscribe below. I'll email you when there's a new post — (look, an Em dash) usually once a week, sometimes less. No spam, no sales pitches.

And if you'd rather just read product updates, the [changelog](/changelog) is where they live.

Thanks for being here. More soon.
